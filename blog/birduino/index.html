Birduino: A Tonuino-based birdcall learning game

8 Jul 2026

Here’s the project write-up for an interactive birdcall learning game I made, using a deck of bird cards, audio from the Cornell Ornithology Lab, RFID stickers, and an open-source RFID-triggered mp3 player project called the Tonuino.

Why?

A few years ago I was excited to discover the Tonuino project, an open-source alternative to the proprietary Yoto Player. While my nephews have a Yoto player, and I love making them mix tapes cards for it, I was curious about the world of open-source alternatives.

At the time the Tonuino inventor was selling component kits, and I purchased a couple. I then lost track of them in my box of electronics. When I came across them earlier this year, I wanted a scoped and self-contained use case for building one of them. Making a birdcall learning tool seemed like a low-effort way to try the kit out. After all, it was just a matter of soldering the kit’s pieces together, right? As the kids say, lol.1

Enclosure

I created a basic box housing in parametric CAD program cuttle.xyz, and then created a second box plus tray so that the assembled Tonuino and its associated bird cards could be presented together.

The Cuttle designs are here and here, if you want to cut your own.

I cut the pieces from 1/8” acrylic, since I think objects with visible innards look cool and hadn’t yet internalized that I’d used an excessive amount of wire relative to the size of the enclosure. This fabrication was done with the laser cutter at my public library’s maker space.

I had to tweak the design to make sure that the speaker and potentiometer fit perfectly, and then had to re-cut the box to leave more space for all the wires.

Even though the enclosure is technically a press-fit box, it needed to be glued for longevity. The choice of clear acrylic made for a frustrating adhesive process, as I needed to find a glue that didn’t make the acrylic cloudy. After way too many different purchases and ugly test bonds, we2 settled on TODO.3

If I were going to build another one, I’d make different wire choices to make the Birduino contents look a little cleaner, but I am happy with how this first attempt turned out.
